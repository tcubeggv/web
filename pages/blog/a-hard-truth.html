A Hard Truth : The Market Rewards Where You Work, Not Just How Hard You Work

← Back to Blog

I used to believe the simple formula we were all sold: grind harder, put in more hours, and success will inevitably follow. But after observing the trajectories of different professionals and founders, I’ve come to a different conclusion. Hard work is just the baseline prerequisite. What actually dictates your ceiling isn’t your sweat.It is the market value of your chosen domain and the your timing.

The Domain Multiplier

Think of your personal effort as a base number, say, 100. The domain you choose is the multiplier. Consider two highly skilled project managers. One manages traditional commercial construction projects, while the other manages projects in an emerging tech field like AI or cloud infrastructure. Both might work 60-hour weeks, manage complex teams, and execute flawlessly under immense stress. Yet, the tech professional often commands a significantly higher salary, better margins, and massive equity upside for the exact same amount of effort. It isn’t because the tech manager works harder. It is because they are applying their effort to a domain with infinite scalability and explosive capital flow. The market does not reward you based on how many hours you worked; it rewards the scarcity and scale of the industry you are in.

The Timing Factor

But choosing the right domain is only half the battle. Even in a high-leverage industry, when you enter matters just as much as where you are. This timing isn’t just “luck”,it’s strategic positioning. Idealab founder Bill Gross analyzed 200 startups to find out why some succeed and others fail. It turns out, timing was the single biggest factor, accounting for 42% of the difference between success and failure. It easily outweighed the execution of the team (32%), the brilliance of the idea (28%), and the business model (24%). Launch too early, and you burn capital trying to educate a market that isn’t ready. Launch too late, and you are crushed by established giants. Real success comes from catching the wave,building today for where the world will be in three years.

Why Entrepreneurship is the Way Out

This realization is exactly why I believe so strongly in entrepreneurship. Entrepreneurship is not a shortcut to bypass hard work by finding a clever market arbitrage. A traditional career often locks you into an employer’s predetermined domain and timeline, capping your upside. Entrepreneurship is the vehicle that allows you to manually override those constraints. It is the conscious decision to evaluate the leverage of your domain, time your market entry, and apply maximum effort toward problems that offer outsized returns. Pick the right domain and time your entry precisely, but accept that once you get there, the actual story of your company will be written entirely by how hard and how fast you execute.
